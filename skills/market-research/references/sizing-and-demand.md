# Demand signals and market sizing

## Demand signals (strongest first)
| Signal | Where | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Paid demand | marketplace bestseller ranks, review counts and review velocity (reviews/month ≈ a fraction of sales), public company revenues, app store ranks | many reviews on several sellers = proven demand; one dominant seller = strong incumbent |
| Search demand | Google Trends (relative interest, seasonality, regions), keyword tools for volume, marketplace search suggestions | rising or stable interest; seasonality matters for stock and cash flow |
| Conversation | Reddit/forums/Ekşi Sözlük-type communities, social hashtags, Q&A sites, YouTube comments | pains and words customers use; weak for volume |
| Trade data | national statistics (TÜİK, Eurostat, Census), customs/trade data (Eurostat Comext, UN Comtrade, ITC Trade Map), industry associations | market totals, import/export flows, prices per unit for export markets |

## Sizing
**Bottom-up (primary):**
```
SOM/yr = reachable target customers × % who buy from you per year × purchases per year × average net price
```
Example (all inputs ~ and must be sourced/assumed explicitly): 2,000,000 reachable households × 5% buying from you × 2 items/yr × 375 TL net ≈ 75 M TL/yr — then ask:
is 5% realistic for a new brand? Usually far lower in year 1; show a range (e.g. 0.1%–1% → 0.75–7.5 M TL).

**Top-down (cross-check):** total category spend (statistics/industry reports) × share of the segment × share of the channel × realistic share for you.

**TAM / SAM / SOM:** TAM = everyone who could buy the category; SAM = the segment and geography you can serve with your model; SOM = what you can win in 1–3 years.
Investors care about the logic; operators care about SOM and the path to it.

## Sanity checks
Compare SOM with competitors of similar size (their revenues, review counts) · check capacity (can you produce/ship that volume?) ·
seasonality (peak month vs average) · price elasticity (premium vs mass segment).
