# Admin and money

General information and organization, not financial, tax or legal advice; point to professionals for decisions with legal/tax consequences.

## Bills and subscriptions
- Build a list: provider, amount, frequency, renewal date, payment method, cancellation terms. Sources: bank/card statements the user shares, email receipts.
- Flag: duplicates, unused subscriptions, price increases, free trials about to convert, annual renewals coming up (remind 2 weeks ahead).
- Cancelling or changing plans → user approves; give step-by-step instructions or do it via connected tools only with approval.

## Simple budget
Categorize the last 1–3 months of spending; show income vs fixed costs vs variable spending; frameworks like 50/30/20 as a starting point, not a rule;
suggest specific, realistic changes; savings goals with monthly amounts and dates. Keep financial data private and local.

## Paperwork and renewals
Track expiry/renewal dates: ID/passport, driving licence, vehicle inspection and insurance, home insurance, domain names, professional licences, warranties.
Remind with enough lead time for appointments. Keep a document index (what, where stored, expiry) — not copies of sensitive documents in chat.

## Forms and official processes
Explain steps and required documents from official sources (dated); pre-fill drafts from information the user provides; the user submits and signs.
Never enter the user's e-government or banking credentials.

## Shopping and comparisons
1. Clarify needs (must-haves, nice-to-haves, budget, deadline).
2. Compare 3–5 options in a table: price (with date and shop), key specs, warranty/returns, delivery time, reviews (watch for fake-review patterns), total cost of ownership.
3. Recommend one with the reason; mention when waiting for a known sale makes sense.
4. Purchase only after approval; prefer trusted sellers; warn about deals that look too good to be true.
