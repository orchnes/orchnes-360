# Acquisition, conversion and experiments

## Paid acquisition
- Start with one or two channels where the audience is (e.g. Meta/Instagram for visual products, Google Shopping/Search for purchase intent, marketplace ads for marketplace sellers).
- Set a **target CAC** from unit economics (`market-research`); small daily budgets; test creatives and audiences in a structured way (one variable per test).
- Track by channel and campaign with UTM parameters; server-side conversion tracking where available; respect consent (tracking only after consent where required).
- Attribution is imperfect — compare against blended CAC (total marketing spend / new customers) and holdout tests when spend grows.
- Kill rules: stop ad sets that exceed target CAC after a pre-set spend; scale winners gradually (e.g. +20–30% budget steps).

## Organic and partnerships
Referral programme (give/get), collaborations with complementary brands, micro-creators with disclosed partnerships, PR/local press, marketplaces as discovery channels.

## Conversion rate optimization
Common wins: faster pages, clear photos and size/capacity info, visible shipping cost/time early, trust signals (reviews, secure payment badges, return policy, real contact info),
guest checkout, local payment methods, fewer form fields, clear error messages, mobile-first layout.

## A/B testing
1. Hypothesis: "Showing delivery date on product pages will raise add-to-cart rate because uncertainty about delivery is a top objection."
2. Primary metric and guardrails (e.g. conversion up, refund rate not up).
3. **Sample size** calculated before starting (baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, significance and power) — small shops often lack traffic for small effects; test bold changes or use before/after with care.
4. Run full weeks; don't peek and stop early; one test per page area at a time.
5. Record results (win/loss/inconclusive) and learnings.

## Avoid
Fake urgency/countdowns, hidden fees revealed at checkout, pre-ticked consent boxes, subscription traps, fake reviews — unlawful in many jurisdictions (EU UCPD/DSA, consumer laws) and damaging to trust.
