# Structure by growth plan

| Plan for next 12–24 months | Usually fits | Why | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testing an idea / side income, solo | sole proprietorship (or freelancer/self-employed status) | cheapest, fastest, simple accounting | unlimited personal liability; may hit tax brackets as income grows |
| Solo full-time, growing revenue | limited company / LLC | limited liability, tax planning, more credible to B2B buyers | higher admin and accounting cost |
| Two or more founders | limited company / LLC + **shareholder agreement** | clear ownership, vesting, decisions, exits | never start partners without a written agreement |
| Raising investment (angels/VC) | joint-stock company / corporation (investor-standard type in that country) | share classes, option pools, easier transfers | legal costs; investors may require specific jurisdictions |
| Selling internationally online | home-country company + registrations abroad (VAT/OSS, marketplaces) | avoids premature foreign entities | permanent-establishment and tax nexus rules |
| Operating teams/stock abroad | subsidiary or branch in that country | local contracts, payments, employment | transfer pricing, double-tax treaties, local compliance |
| Group with several businesses | holding structure | separates risk, dividends, exits | only when there's real value; adds cost |

## Conversion path
Most countries allow converting sole proprietorship → limited → joint-stock, or contributing a business into a new company. Note what changes at each step
(tax IDs, contracts, bank accounts, licences, VAT numbers, marketplace and payment accounts) so the move is planned, not chaotic.

## Founder checklist (multi-founder)
Equity split with reasoning · vesting (e.g. 4 years, 1-year cliff) · roles and decision rights · deadlock resolution · leaver terms ·
IP assignment to the company · non-compete/non-solicit where enforceable · option pool for future hires.
