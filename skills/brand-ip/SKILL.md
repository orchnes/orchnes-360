# Brand & IP

Search before you fall in love with a name; secure ownership before you invest in a brand; file before you disclose an invention.
IP rules and fees are country-specific and change — verify with official offices (`research`) and confirm strategy with an IP attorney.

## 1. Inventory what needs protection
| Asset | Usual protection | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Brand name, logo, slogan, packaging look | trademark | `references/trademark.md` |
| Product shape/appearance (e.g. a ceramic form, a pattern) | industrial design registration (+ unregistered design rights where they exist) | `references/patents-designs.md` |
| Technical invention | patent or utility model | `references/patents-designs.md` |
| Photos, texts, illustrations, software, logo artwork | copyright (automatic) + **written assignment** to the company | `references/patents-designs.md` |
| Domains, social handles, app names, marketplace stores | registrations and platform brand registries | `references/digital-assets.md` |
| Recipes, processes, customer lists, source code not published | trade secrets (NDAs, access control) | `references/digital-assets.md` |
| Third-party code/fonts/images you use | licence compliance | `references/digital-assets.md` |

## 2. Clearance (before choosing or launching a name/logo)
1. Distinctiveness check — invented or arbitrary names are strongest; descriptive names are weak and often refused.
2. Identical and similar marks in relevant classes in target countries (official databases), company registries, domains, handles, marketplaces, general web search.
3. Report risk as low / medium / high with the conflicting marks found (links, classes, status) and what was **not** searched.
A clearance search by an agent is a screening, not a legal opinion; high-value brands get an attorney's search and opinion.

## 3. Strategy
Countries where you sell/manufacture/plan to expand within ~3 years · classes covering goods and services you actually offer · filing route
(national first, then international within the priority period, or regional EU filing) · word mark first, logo mark when the design is final ·
budget per country and timeline (~, dated).

## 4. Ownership hygiene (cheap, often forgotten)
Founders assign IP to the company · designers and freelancers sign copyright assignments (logo files and rights, not just the PNG) ·
employees' contracts cover IP · AI-generated elements are human-refined and documented (AI-only output may not be protectable).

## 5. After registration
Use the mark consistently (™/® rules per country) · keep proof of use · monitor new filings and marketplaces (watch service or periodic searches) ·
oppose conflicting applications within deadlines · renew on time (calendar reminders) · enroll in marketplace brand registries · act on infringements (takedown → cease-and-desist via attorney → litigation as last resort).

## 6. Human approval
Filing applications, paying fees, signing powers of attorney/assignments, sending cease-and-desist letters, responding to office actions or oppositions:
agent prepares, owner decides and signs, IP attorney advises (mandatory for foreign applicants in some countries).

## Rules
- Never declare a name "available" or "safe" — report search results, gaps and risk level with dates.
- Don't publish an invention (website, crowdfunding, trade fair, paper) before patent advice.
- Never copy competitors' marks, trade dress or designs; similarity analysis is for avoiding conflict.
